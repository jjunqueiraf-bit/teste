# NASA sources used, and exactly what each one changed

Listed for the Validity judging lens: a NASA source, a traceable rule, a tested
outcome. Everything below is used in the game, not just cited.

## 1. Design and Develop Science Missions
NASA, Access to Space for All — Systems Engineering Webinar Series,
Julie Levri, Flight Systems Engineer, 26 March 2025.
`https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/250323-design-and-develop-science-mission.pdf`

**What it gave us, verbatim:**
- The six-step process: **Identify Need & Goals → Define Concept → Define
  Requirements → Design → Build & Verify → Implement**
- The four segments of a science mission:
  - *Launch segment:* launch vehicle and related services
  - *Space segment:* spacecraft, orbiters, landers, rovers and instruments
  - *Ground segment:* ground stations and mission control that receive data
  - *User segment:* scientists and the public who use the data

**Where it appears in the game:** the six steps are the progress bar across the
top of every design screen. The four segments are how BUILD & VERIFY summarises
the player's mission back to them.

## 2. CubeSat 101
NASA CubeSat Launch Initiative.
`https://www3.nasa.gov/sites/default/files/atoms/files/nasa_csli_cubesat_101_508.pdf`

- "A 1U CubeSat is a 10 cm cube with a mass of approximately 1 to 1.33 kg"
- ~$300,000 is "typically enough to launch a 3U into low-Earth orbit"

**Where it appears:** anchors the mass and cost scales so the numbers a player
sees are the right order of magnitude.

## 3. DONKI — Space Weather Database of Notifications, Knowledge, Information
`https://api.nasa.gov/DONKI/CME`

132 real coronal mass ejections, 1 Oct – 14 Nov 2026. Each in-game storm uses
its real **speed** (218–1048 km/s), real **halfAngle** (10–46°, giving 64
glancing / 36 partial / 32 direct hits) and NASA's own **class** (S/C/O).
Archived and labelled as archived.

## 4. NASA Image and Video Library
`https://images-api.nasa.gov`

Ten credited photographs, each shown with title, date and NASA ID — including
PIA26368, the actual 'leopard spots' rock, and as17-145-22157, Apollo 17
scooping lunar regolith.

## Orbital mechanics used in the launch-window step
Earth orbits in 365 days, Mars in 687, so a low-energy alignment occurs roughly
every **26 months**. A Hohmann transfer takes about **259 days** and needs about
**3.6 km/s**. The current window runs late Nov 2026 – early Jan 2027; the next
is 2029. The Moon, by contrast, is reachable every few days with a ~3 day transit.

## Power figures
An MMRTG produces roughly **110 W** of electrical power and keeps producing it
at night and through dust storms. Mars receives about **43%** of the sunlight
Earth does, before dust. These set the power budget that can block a launch.
